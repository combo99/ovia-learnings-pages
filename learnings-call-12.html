Learnings Call #12

Two breakthrough winners and two ads that never spent, and the difference between them came down to one thing: whether there was a single idea the viewer could point to.

4 batches reviewed · CB25 + CB22 broke through · #98 and #102 never got spend

✂️ Built for editors — focused, do-this-now takeaways
Promise the cravings are gone. Let them do the rest of the math.

Every winner this call sold one simple promise and one simple mechanism. Every loser stacked several good ideas and left nothing to point at. Simpler is not a compromise here — it is the thing that converts.

01

What showed up in all four batches

The same three rules explain both wins and both losses. If you only take three things into this week's work, take these.

ONE big idea
Insulin resistance, strawberry acai + cravings, removing all hunger, or GLP-1 — pick one and only one. If it can't be said in a single phrase, it isn't ready.
Cravings, not the visual before/after
Promise zero cravings. The physical result belongs in the ad as proof, never as the lead claim — the moment you lead with it, belief collapses.
SHOW the not-hungry reveal
Don't say she isn't hungry — put it on screen. Refusing the drive-thru, pushing away the donut, turning down the turkey.

🎯Why "cravings" out-converts "belly fat"

Khush's teardown of batch #98, and it reframes everything else on this page.

  • These women have already tried eating less. Batch #98 essentially says eat less and the belly fat goes — which is the exact thing that has already failed them. Nothing new is revealed, so nothing is believed.
  • CB25 and CB22 stop at cravings. They promise no cravings and nothing else. Women in their 50s and 60s still bought — because they complete the chain: no cravings → I eat less → I lose the weight.
  • A simple promise needs no complex mechanism. Promise a physical change and the mechanism has to be sophisticated enough to carry it. Promise no cravings and one line of mechanism is plenty.
  • Cravings is a broader problem than belly fat. Binge eaters, midnight snackers, women worried about blood sugar — all of them want the same thing.

Careful: the bigger desire is still the physical change. This is about what we lead with, not what we deliver.

🏷️Name the mechanism so it can be pictured

  • "You lost the insulin sensitivity she has" is unvisualizable. Batch #102's hook gave the viewer nothing to see, and there was nothing on screen helping either.
  • Compare "parasites." One word and everyone pictures the worm. Same with "kneecap grinding," or the EMMA VSL's "you don't have constipation, you have poop paralysis."
  • Use the doctor/me split. "Doctors call it insulin sensitivity, I call it this" — instantly separates us from the medical mumbo jumbo and signals I'm one of you.
  • Body-part language beats lab language. Menopause belly reads; insulin resistance does not.
02

The winning flow

CB25 and CB22 are different formats running the same skeleton. This is the structure to plug old batches into.

🧱The three-step spine — SHOW → STARBUCKS → MECHANISM

  • 1. Show it, or open on drama. Either the not-hungry reveal on screen (CB22: turning down Chipotle and Bojangles) or a negative, ambiguous moment that pulls people in — the spiked drink, "my girlfriend is starving herself."
  • 2. Make it familiar through Starbucks. The drink is introduced as a strawberry acai refresher, mixed in a Starbucks cup. Familiar container, novel twist — not another berberine supplement, the first ever refresher that controls cravings.
  • 3. Then, and only then, the mechanism. One cause, stated simply. CB25 uses 5-HTP and serotonin and nothing else. The mechanism is only the justification for why the drink works — the taste and the ease do the selling.

Leo: swap the order of the mechanism and the Starbucks introduction and see what happens — if the "it's just a Starbucks drink" frame lands first, everything after it should feel easier to accept.

🧾Proof first, and push it past "it worked"

  • CB25 leads with proof before the product exists in the ad. "I literally have to force myself to eat after taking this." "I had to get my brother's wedding dress altered because it's now too big on me — but it fit two weeks ago."
  • The technique is "it worked too well." Not the product worked — the product worked so well it caused a problem. Think of the worst thing that could happen if you stopped eating, then tune it back to the sweet spot.
  • Contrast the pain with the ease at the end. CB25 twists the knife — insecure in a bikini this summer — and then answers it with a packet in her purse. The gap between those two is what pushes the buy.

🔍Build sub-avatars from the problem, not the demographic

  • Start with the problem. Search binge eating, binge eating disorder, emotional eater. Read the threads. The sub-avatars surface on their own — women in their 50s and women in their 20s saying the same thing.
  • Don't research with the avatar already in mind. Deciding on "gym girl" and then looking up gym girls only confirms what you already assumed.
  • Keep it broad. The problem is the same across ages; what changes is how it shows up. A 20-year-old wants to look good for her boyfriend, a 50-year-old cares about blood sugar and diabetes.
03

Batch teardowns

Two losers first, because they diagnose the winners. Both losers ran on small spend — treat the numbers as directional.

🧩Batch #98 — Thanking The Woman That Showed OVIA low spend iteration of CB27-C

Frame.io ↗

Hypothesis: CB27-C was getting a lot of spend but was badly executed from a DR point of view — soft metrics solid, CVR low. Take the same idea and raise belief with a coherent mechanism without overcomplicating it.

What went wrong
  • The mechanism became a mix of everything. A bit of each idea, nothing singular to point at — the hypothesis was not confirmed.
  • Good hook rate and good CTR, not enough sales. A belief problem, not an attention problem: they watch, they click, they don't buy.
  • It promises the visual result. The first frame is a flat stomach, so we're claiming belly fat gone — the claim these women have already watched fail.
  • It never shows the not-hungry reveal. The idea is stated, never depicted.
  • The original won on something this one dropped. CB27-C leaned hard on the GLP-1 comparison, which borrowed authority instantly.
What to keep
  • The older-audience targeting. It was one of our first ads pointed at the older avatar and the direction still looks right.
  • The strawberry refresher comparison. Ease and familiarity is the reason the original converted.
Iterate
→
Rebuild it on the insulin mechanism, kept plain. "Your food is just being stored as fat, that's why the weight sits on your belly and you're exhausted" — then confirm with symptoms. Don't lecture insulin resistance.
→
Rewrite in 50-year-old language. Swap the young-audience references — TikTok becomes a Facebook group.
→
Add a pain-initiation beat. Cravings on its own may not carry enough pain unless she already feels guilt about it.

🧩Batch #102 — "they're just doing X" low spend lost in the first 15s

Frame.io ↗

Hypothesis: mirror the skincare TikTok where she names the viewer's insecurity, tells her the prettier women simply don't have problem X, and then explains it. Our version swaps in insulin sensitivity.

What went wrong
  • The hook can't be pictured. "You lost the insulin sensitivity she has" — you can't visualize it, and nothing on screen helps you.
  • The root cause is too complex. The inspiration used something anyone can see on a face; we used a metabolic term menopausal women don't know.
  • The knife is never twisted. We don't earn the next 15 seconds, so Meta never gives it spend.
  • It points at a problem, not a root cause. And it's stated flatly — no emotion anywhere in the read or the copy.
  • The VO is monotone. Next to CB25, where the voice lifts and drops, both the hook rate and the hold rate suffer.
What to keep
  • The "she's not better than you, she just doesn't have X" frame. The imported structure is sound — the fill-in was wrong.
  • The practitioner authority beat. "My menopause practitioner exposed that 82% of women over 40 secretly develop…" is a workable statistic-that-flips-it moment.
Iterate
→
Rename the root cause. Something visual and instantly understandable, in the poop-paralysis mould, plus B-roll that shows it.
→
Restructure the open. Tease the root cause → name the solutions she already tried, with symptoms → the statistic that flips it → more symptoms to confirm. The parasite playbook.
→
Test a symptoms callout into insulin. Lead with a symptom she already has, then funnel back to insulin as the cause.
→
Cut a Pixar-style side animation. Half the screen: food goes down the food pipe and turns into fat. Comprehension speed is the whole point.

🏆CB25 — Binge Eater Hack breakthrough winner old ad account

Atria ↗
Why it won
  • It promises cravings gone and nothing else. No weight-loss claim, no visual before/after as the lead — and women over 50 and 60 still bought.
  • One mechanism only. 5-HTP and serotonin as the single root cause. No bouncing between GLP-1, yerba mate and everything else.
  • Proof before product. Forcing herself to eat, the wedding dress altered — and the "it fit two weeks ago" line that makes it land.
  • The drink arrives as a Starbucks refresher. "Just grab a little Starbucks water… my fun little pinkity drinkity."
  • Pain contrasted with ease at the end. Insecure in a bikini this summer, versus a packet in her purse.
  • Its own name for the problem. "An emotional snacker" — we can't call women out for snacking directly without offending them, but framed this way they recognise themselves.
What to fix
  • She opens holding the product. Our ads have never posted a strong hook rate when they start on the product — people see it and know they're about to be sold to.
  • It tells the not-hungry reveal instead of showing it. No visual of her refusing food anywhere.
  • The cast skews young for the avatar that's actually buying.
Iterate
→
New hook with no product jar in the opening. Cheapest fix available, already in progress.
→
Exaggerate the results. "I had to stop drinking this, I was losing too much weight." "My mom started calling me sticks." Or the ER version.
→
Horizontally scale by avatar. Same script for a gym girl and for a menopausal woman, wording made congruent to each.
→
Plug the structure into old batches. Batch 42 has a near-identical skeleton — and its iterations may have failed because each one added more instead of taking things out.

🏆CB22 — Girlfriend Starving Herself breakthrough winner skit

Atria ↗
Why it won
  • It opens on something that looks bad. "My girlfriend is starving herself" — the same negative framing as the spiked-drink ad. You're pulled in before you know what this is.
  • It shows the not-hungry reveal. She turns down Chipotle, turns down Bojangles, and the boyfriend confronts her about it.
  • The contradiction is stated out loud. "You're literally drinking that sugary ass drink, but you never want to eat anymore." Polar opposites, set up in the first seconds.
  • She names it herself. "This is literally my craving control" — not the product name, not "a drink."
  • One problem, one mechanism, no complexity. Same spine as CB25 in a completely different format.
What to test next
  • No major weaknesses. This one is about finding more ways to run the same spine, not repairing it.
  • The mechanism is probably swappable. Serotonin to GLP-1 activation would likely still work — the intro sequence, the drama and the showing are what carry it.
Iterate
→
Make the cast older. Same beats, older people, different locations.
→
Find new ways to refuse food. A family gathering where she won't eat with his family is the obvious next one.
→
Turn the skit into a story. Add the pain before and the thing that happened in the middle, so it plays like something you watch rather than something you're sold.
04

Concepts to iterate

Everything agreed on the call, grouped by where it came from. Unclaimed ones are up for grabs.

🥤Off CB22 — drama first, then the reveal

  • Indecisive girlfriend. He hands her the drink before they pick a restaurant, and suddenly she doesn't want to go anywhere.
  • Girlfriend craves junk food. He spikes her drink in the car on the way, and she has no appetite by the time they sit down.
  • Thanksgiving. Grandma's drink gets spiked, the turkey comes out, grandma wants none of it.
  • The wedding. The bride's drink gets spiked and she won't eat her own cake.
  • Ex-husband spikes her drink at the wedding — same beat, maximum controversy.
  • Girlfriend loves eating out, boyfriend never joins her.
  • Family gathering. She won't eat with his family and it becomes a scene — then it turns out she's just drinking this.
  • Boyfriend secretly drinking her pink drink and not understanding why he isn't hungry. Claimed — Mei, in progress, coupled with the Nana character. Reference ad ↗

Keep the spiking ambiguous. Never explain at the start why the drink is being spiked — prank or kindness. Not knowing is the curiosity that holds them.

📋Off the other three batches

  • #98 — insulin rewrite. One plain mechanism, confirmed with symptoms. Claimed — Khush, with Leo.
  • #98 — 50-year-old language pass. Claimed — Mei.
  • #102 — renamed root cause, something you can picture and feel, plus the B-roll that shows it.
  • #102 — parasite-playbook restructure of the opening sequence.
  • #102 — symptoms callout leading into insulin.
  • CB25 — new hook, no product jar in the opening. Claimed — Mei.
  • CB25 — "worked too well" proof variants and the avatar-by-avatar horizontal scale.
