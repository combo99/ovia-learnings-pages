Learnings Call #13

Three iterations that got spend without breaking through, and one old KPI winner pulled back out of the drawer — all four came down to the same gap: we keep saying the drink kills hunger and we almost never show it.

4 batches reviewed · show the reveal, show the ease · the root-cause question finally becomes a test

✂️ For strategists and editors — focused, do-this-now takeaways
We already have the right pieces. Hit them twice as hard and cut the rest.

Leo's one-sentence summary of the whole call: show how it makes people not hungry, dramatise having to force yourself to eat, and hide the product inside a strawberry refresher so it looks effortless. Everything that isn't one of those three is what to cut.

01

What showed up in all four batches

Same three gaps in a losing UGC ad, a losing AI song, an unclear iteration and an old winner. That's what makes them worth turning into constraints for this week.

Show the
not-hungry reveal
Every script claims she isn't hungry. Almost none of them put it on screen — the cookies she doesn't touch, the candy on the seat behind her.
Show the ease
The original wins by being just a strawberry drink you grab like a Starbucks refresher. Ours describe how easy it is instead of showing it.
Exaggerate
or it won't land
"I have to force myself to eat" and "my fridge has been empty for two days" — not "I skipped my afternoon cookies."

🎬The structure the call agreed on

  • Hook. Drama or a confession, no product in frame.
  • Exaggerated results, shown. The food she isn't eating, on camera, before anything is explained.
  • The confrontation. In the Nana script, she confronts the granddaughter — the granddaughter confesses and the mechanism arrives cloaked inside the confession.
  • Future pacing. Steadier on the stairs, groceries in one trip, energy to do things — the pains from the survey data, at the end rather than the front.
  • CTA and risk reversal.

Keep the loop open: both losing batches resolved themselves in the opening lines — one forgave the granddaughter before the story started, the other gave away "I came back in a size 4". Lymphoria's ads tease the ending and never spoil it.

🎵Native first, song second

  • A concept that works as a native doesn't automatically work as an AI song, and the reverse is just as true. Khush has had natives ripping on another brand die as songs.
  • So the order is fixed: test the story as a native ad, and only build the AI-song version once the native works.
  • Copying a competitor's story verbatim is the risky version. Lymphoria can afford roughly 0.4 ROAS on 10–12% COGS, so we get outbid on their own story. Taking their structure and putting our mechanism in it, as a native, is the version that stays novel.
02

The mechanism question — now a test

The longest argument of the call, and it ended with both sides agreeing to run it rather than settle it.

⚖️Do we need a root cause at all?

The case for no mechanism
  • It's what's working right now. The native winner does nothing but say the drink makes her instantly not hungry.
  • Fewer BS detectors, not more. Everyone already believes that if you don't eat, you lose weight — an invented mechanism is the part they argue with.
  • Novelty. The market has seen appetite suppressants, never "a drink that makes you instantly not hungry".
  • Ease may be the real sell. Half the buyers in the survey said they just wanted to try it.
The case against
  • It may not scale. The pool of "I'll try anything" women is smaller than the market as a whole, so pure claims might cap out before a 100K–200K ad.
  • Desperation isn't belief. Buyers may be converting despite scepticism rather than because they're convinced.
  • Competitors take the blame away. Resilient blames parasites, Lymphoria blames the lymphatic system — we blame nothing.
How it gets settled
→
Run both. One batch that calls out the perimenopause symptoms and credits only no-hunger plus faster metabolism, against a variant that explains the root cause.

🔍From the survey research

  • Lowest-hanging fruit is general weight gain in women 60–70 who don't identify with menopause and still read it as a willpower problem.
  • Problem language splits hard by age band. 20–29: being the biggest in the room, thighs wearing out the crotch of their pants. 60–70+: getting up out of a chair, a knee that won't take it, not being able to walk alone.
  • GLP-1 is borrowed authority, not an alternative they're shopping for. Buyers aren't looking for a cheaper GLP-1 — they're refusing needles, and will take a natural supplement indefinitely.
  • The older audience already believes the body heals itself if it's given the right ingredients, which is the belief the 60+ scripts should sit on.
  • Postpartum is an open avatar — moms who have stopped breastfeeding and still can't shift the weight.
03

Batch teardowns

Three of these launched 8/2 and had three days of data at call time, so read the numbers as directional, not settled.

🧩Batch#131 — Older Mom Binge Eating unclear $914.82 · ROAS 1.82 · V3

Iteration of CB25: the proven binge-eating winner with the avatar swapped to an older mom. Mei (strategist / editor) · 50+ woman · problem aware.

What went wrong
  • It drifted from the original. Lines the winner never had — "keep in mind I'm 49, I work full time", "trust me, you need it" — went in, and the serotonin mechanism arrives with no bridge from the symptoms before it.
  • A logical inconsistency in the hook. "I barely have time to eat sitting down" tells the viewer she already eats less, which cancels the problem the product solves.
  • Hot flashes are in the copy and the product does nothing for them. Tired and exhausted are the honest swaps.
  • Product in the hook. Even a glimpse of the jar drags the hook rate down.
  • Summer as the urgency anchor, with summer already over.
What to keep
  • The older avatar is the right bet — the objection it answers came straight from the comments on the top spender.
  • The hook is lifted from the original winner, and that's the part that's working.
  • "Binge eating hack" is a real entry point — it's just never paid off in the body of the ad.
Iterate
→
Stick to the original script. Change the avatar, keep everything else the winner had.
→
Say the real problem out loud. "I'm constantly craving sweets during work hours", "I put away four cookies a day" — not "I have no time".
→
Show her refusing food. Chocolate in the car she doesn't want, candy on the seats behind her.
→
Anchor urgency to a real event. Thanksgiving and Christmas gatherings, or "you've tried everything and nothing worked".

🧩Batch#125 — Nana UGC losing $201.57 · ROAS 1.68 · V2

Batch#88 turned into a story: the granddaughter has been spiking her drink. Khush (strategist) / Utkarsh (editor) · 50+ woman · problem aware.

What went wrong
  • The payoff is given away in the first two lines. "I've forgiven her" closes the loop before the story starts — cut that line and the script still works.
  • Too much was changed at once, so it stopped being a clean iteration of #88 and started testing a second hypothesis at the same time.
  • No failed solutions. These women have been through Weight Watchers, Atkins and everything else; the script reads like her first ever weight problem.
  • The problem language isn't hers. The survey says "I'm running out of breath", "I want to play with my grandkids".
  • The hunger effect is undersold. "I stopped eating my afternoon cookies" instead of "I have to force myself to eat".
  • She never shows it. The cookies she hasn't touched are described, never held up.
What to keep
  • The entry point is genuinely new and it's already getting spend — there's no reason this can't become a winner.
  • The hook works. The spiked drink and the confrontation are drama the viewer has to stay for.
  • You don't need to explain why she did it. Leaving the reason out is part of why the spiking ads work.
Iterate
→
Rebuild on the agreed structure. Hook, exaggerated results shown, confront the granddaughter, mechanism inside the confession, future pacing, CTA.
→
Run a plain pain-agitate-solution variant against it. Already written — it's the control for the new structure.
→
Move the pains to the back. Holding the rail, stopping halfway with the groceries — as future pacing, not as setup.

🧩Batch#130 — B28 Wedding, AI song losing $56.43 · ROAS 1.17 · V3

The wedding-dress story as an AI song, modelled on what's printing for the top brands. Mei (strategist / editor) · 50+ woman · unaware · imitation.

What went wrong
  • The mechanism is the wrong one. The current native winner does nothing except say the drink makes her instantly not hungry.
  • It's rushed. The competitor's story ads run several minutes; this one compresses the reveal, the mechanism and the ingredients into a couple of lines.
  • No breaking point. She binges with no build-up — the binge should read as a relapse after everything else failed, which is what makes the discovery land.
  • The character has no depth, so there's nothing for the viewer to step into. Stories work by lending an experience.
  • The first line may be backwards. "I was never the skinniest in the room" — the research says these women could eat anything until menopause and woke up in a body that stopped responding.
  • The ending is spoiled early with "I came back in a size 4".
What to keep
  • It is genuinely top of funnel. You don't need to know anything about the product to buy into the story, and the product doesn't appear until halfway.
  • The big-idea template is reusable: a dramatic breaking-point event — the husband who left, the mother-in-law — and a story arc built around it.
Iterate
→
Native first. Any Lymphoria-style story gets tested as a native ad; the song version only follows a native that works.
→
Map the wedding idea onto the working Lymphoria arc rather than compressing our own version of it.
→
Build the relapse. Defeat, resignation, "fuck it" — then the discovery.

🏆CB63 — Drink This To Get Ready For Summer KPI winner $8,002.67 · ROAS 1.62 · 25 days

An old-ad-account winner pulled back out to re-read the principles that made it work. Ran until the account ban.

Why it worked
  • Exaggerated outcome plus visual proof up front — lifting her shirt in the opening is some of the best proof we've had.
  • Ease, hammered. "The easiest way to a bikini body fast", "the lazy girl hack", "this is all I've been drinking" — twice at the end, and throughout.
  • A specific, repeatable line: "I have to take this every morning or I literally won't eat the entire day." It tested well as a hook on its own.
  • Clinically proven framing on the yerba mate, which may be what carries a science-minded viewer.
What held it back
  • It's all claims and no proof — the comments asked for a before picture, not just a stomach shot.
  • She says it's easy without showing it, and never puts the drink in a Starbucks cup where it would read as a strawberry refresher.
  • She says it kills appetite without showing it.
  • No explanation of how the cravings disappear — metabolism and cravings are asserted and dropped.
  • "Bikini body" may be the wrong desire above roughly 55, where the pull is closer to "this is who I used to be".
Iterate
→
Rerun it on the Batch#42 avatar — around 60 — with the before/after added, the cravings explained, and the ease and the appetite kill both shown.
→
Swap "all the hot girls" for low-key authority. Photos of recognisable TV faces drinking a pink drink, never naming the product — the way Resilient used Kevin Gates.
