Show them not hungry. The mechanism is just the belief bridge.

Every ad that is spending does the same thing in a different costume. The job this week is new ways to show the outcome, not better explanations of it.

01

What showed up in every batch

Three things repeated across all four teardowns. If you only change three things in your cuts this week, change these.

Older woman
on camera
Every ad that is spending has one. Meta is serving these to 55+, and older women buy even when the character is young.
Show it,
don't say it
The candy bowl he never reaches for. The plate she won't finish. The more literally it is shown, the better it works.
Product out
of the lens
Retention drops on the exact frame the packet goes up to camera — not on the mention of the product.

🔁The pattern under all four batches

  • The outcome is the sell. They are buying the instant, visible result — she drank it, she is not hungry. Mechanism exists only to make that believable.
  • Relatability beats polish. The hook that beat every "are you on GLP-1s?" variant was "is anybody else's grandma like this?" It does not feel like an ad.
  • Borrowed authority has to already exist. We channel drama people are already arguing about. We cannot invent a person nobody is talking about.
  • Believability is built before the product appears. By the time the product shows up, the people who will buy have already stayed.

Reconciling two notes that look contradictory: "introduce the product more" (Batch#114 sells harder and converts) and "the product kills retention" are the same finding from two sides — sell harder with words, not with the packet in frame. One setup line before it appears, and never hold it to the lens.

02

This week's tests

Binary constraints, not suggestions. Each one should come back with a clear yes or no by next call.

⏱️Tease the not-hungry reveal before the video starts

Cut the reveal line to the very front, hold it no longer than 1.5 seconds, then hard-cut into the real opening. Two hooks stacked tight — at three seconds it stops being a tease and becomes the video.

→
Run it against the untweaked hook. Referenced in Batch#114 and Batch#103.

📦The product never comes up to the lens

One cut per batch keeps the product readable but out of the held-to-camera position all the way through the explanation. Conceal it the way real UGC does.

→
Watch the retention graph at the product frame. Referenced in Batch#114 and CB162-E.

👁️The before/after has to read at a glance

No day-by-day timeline cut goes out unless the first and final frames read as two visibly different bodies side by side — and still fully believable. Batch#103 failed on exactly this.

→
More drastic, still real. Referenced in Batch#103.

🧪One unified mechanism sentence vs the stacked version

Any batch shipping multiple variants runs one stacked-mechanism variant against one single-sentence variant. Educating mechanism by mechanism and bundling it at the end carries more cognitive load than one causal chain.

→
Scripting decision, not an edit call. Referenced in CB37-D and Batch#114.
03

The mechanism playbook

CB37-D is the reference. Copy the structure, not the words — anyone who knows the science will catch that GLP-1 does not literally activate serotonin.

🧠Why the CB37-D version works

  • It unifies instead of stacking. GLP-1 activation → serotonin in the brain → cravings gone. One chain, one thing to understand.
  • Two mechanisms mean two things to process. "GLP-1 curbs cravings AND serotonin stops bored eating" doubles the load for no extra belief.
  • It is three sentences long. Keep it to roughly one line. If it needs a second breath, it is too much.
  • Then it future-paces. "Your jawline's looking a little sharper" does more work than another ingredient.

The open question: magnesium-for-stress at the end — does it stack perceived value, or is it one detail past the click? Split-tested this week, not decided.

Batch teardowns

Spend was thin on several of these — treat the reads as directional, not settled. Batch#103 in particular drew its conclusions off very little delivery.

🧩Batch#107 — spiking drink, younger avatar no spend never exited learning

Frame.io ↗
What went wrong
  • Too similar to what we already ran. It competes with Batch#88, #100 and #104 for the same delivery, with nothing new to offer.
  • Wrong avatar for the mechanism. PCOS points at a younger woman, but Meta is serving to 55+, where the focus shifts to menopause.
  • Spiking a young woman's drink is less interesting than spiking a nana's — the top spender got "great nana acting" comments, this got none.
  • The reaction lands too early. She takes one bite and he immediately questions her, with no cut to signal time passing.
  • It drags. A split second of silence when she returns from the bathroom and sits down, right on "here's your entree."
What to keep
  • The hook itself was fine. Hook rate was not the failure — delivery and sameness were.
Iterate
→
Speed it to ~1.5x in CapCut. Pull the audio forward; AI footage runs slow by default.
→
Recast older. Align the character with who Meta actually delivers to.
→
If we keep PCOS, go solution-aware. "Stop taking metformin daily — there's a natural alternative." Stage 4/5 sophistication market.
→
Swap the insulin-resistance line for authority through the girls: "I heard PCOS girls talking about it on a podcast."

🧩Batch#114 — not spiking a drink spending like the top spender Mei

What to improve
  • Retention dips the instant the product goes up to the lens and stays up through the whole mechanism explanation.
  • The product intro is abrupt. It jumps straight into what it is and starts selling with no setup.
  • It starts slow — the first beats need tightening before the reveal.
  • Wardrobe drift. Her jacket morphs from light grey to dark grey across the video.
What is making it work
  • An older woman again. Same demographic as every other winner.
  • She sells harder and explains more — fewer people stay, but the ones who do actually convert. It lands at the same CPA from the opposite direction.
  • The hook is stupid simple. "Is anybody else's grandma like this?" beat every GLP-1 callout hook.
  • It does not feel like an ad in the first half.
Iterate
→
One setup line before the product. "My nurse recommended this and I've been taking it" — authority plus curiosity, reads educational instead of salesy.
→
Stitch it to the original nana ad. Run the original through "I'm not hungry anymore", slight transition, then this ad's explanation. Ignore that it doesn't quite make sense.
→
Two grandmas, camera flip. Needs two genuinely distinct voices — voice changer or different accents.

🧩CB162-E — organic content format some spend borrowed authority

What to improve
  • It is too long. Only a fraction of viewers reach the end, and the social proof lands after most have gone.
  • The "for you page" tangent takes far too long to make one point: this is everywhere.
  • The ingredient rundown is weak. "Berberine, yerba mate, if you know you know" is not doing the work.
  • The order is wrong. Product hype → seen-it-everywhere → pains → product again.
  • A man is delivering a woman's story — the pains he lists are the ones menopausal women describe.
What to keep
  • Authority we did not invent. Huda from Love Island already has drama attached; the ad just connects the dots to the product.
  • FOMO framing. "At this point, who's NOT taking this?"
  • He mirrors the skeptic's internal dialogue. "I always thought it was just another bullshit TikTok shop product."
  • He shows he is not snacking — candy in close proximity, untouched.
  • The product intro does not feel like an ad at all.
Iterate
→
Reorder it. Authority hype → her pains → product and mechanism → risk reversal.
→
Recut with female AI UGC. It is a women's product; a guy telling it is the ceiling.
→
Find an older celebrity with the same drama. Lands the format on our winning demo.

🧩Batch#103 — day-by-day timeline very low spend 44% hook rate avg play 0:12

Frame.io — v3 ↗
What made it lose
  • The before/after is not visible. First frame and last frame look like the same person — and the whole idea rested on showing the change.
  • Wrong person holding the camera. A timeline documented by someone else is not believable; the grandson would have to be there constantly.
  • The hook is weaker than the other spiking-drink variations, and the winning ones were set in restaurants.
  • Bystanders break it. With others at the table, nobody would miss him spiking her drink.
Almost all of the spend behind this read sat on a single variation. Treat these conclusions as directional.
What to keep
  • The visual colour change. Clear water turning pink reads as "spiked" instantly, and those hooks outperformed.
  • Spiking it right in front of her drives curiosity — is she going to notice?
Iterate
→
Make the before/after unmistakable. The Primal Queen ad is the standard — you cannot believe it is the same person, and it never says so out loud.
→
Set day 1 in a restaurant. Reuse the hook that is already crushing.
→
Hidden camera. He plants it low, spikes it while she is away, grabs it and leaves.
→
Scale comparison as unfakeable proof. First day against the last day. You cannot fake the number moving.
→
Name the outcome out loud. Not "what is in this stuff" — "you put this in my drink and I didn't eat that entire day."
