The plan, in one paragraph

Two lanes, nothing else, for the next few weeks. Lane 1 — the big idea: a Starbucks strawberry refresher that makes you instantly not hungry. We have not exhausted this; there are thousands of ways to show someone not being hungry, not fifty. Lane 2 — stories: take the AI-animated dramatic-event stories that are printing for Lymphoria and Resilia and translate them into every format we can — native ads, realistic AI-UGC, talking head, song, paper/clay animation. The realistic AI-UGC story format is the open lane: Resilia has two live, Lymphoria has zero, nobody else is running it. Middle- and bottom-of-funnel waits 2–3 weeks until top-of-funnel volume can feed it.

Decisions locked

DecisionDetail
No root-cause mechanism We deliberately do not explain why they gained weight. Our mechanism is how stupid-simple the product is to take: "you'll have to force yourself to eat after drinking this." More people believe that than believe oregano oil kills the parasites making them fat.
Stories replace the mechanism A story creates the turning point (broke my foot, husband cheated) that a root cause would otherwise have to supply — without insulting the buyer or blaming willpower. This is why story ads matter for us specifically.
Male audience gets a real test Top 4 ads by CVR / ROAS are male-targeted. The cutting-mix ad runs 2.6 ROAS with spend concentrated in 55–64 y/o men — on a funnel built entirely for women. Separate CBO, separate link, $100/day to the product page first. If it holds, we build the whole male funnel out.
Older avatar is the priority segment Their language is quality of life, not looks — walking without losing breath, being outgoing again. Not "look your best."
Survey data is directional only The post-purchase survey is a confirmation bias of the ads we ran. Read it as a signal, never as the market.
Weekly mix leans iteration / imitation Roughly 1–2 net-new ideations/week from research; the rest iterations and imitations. The old ad account still has unextracted juice. Exact split gets set once Leo publishes hit rates.
CB98-A is the white whale Only ad we've ever had that spends at 100–200K scale. We have still never cracked a winning iteration of it.
Editor turnaround target 8 concepts/week per editor. 2–3 days from copy finished to ad launched. Current lag is the bottleneck on every feedback loop.

Creative direction

Formats to test off a single working story

Native ad · AI animated (Pixar-style) · AI song · AI UGC song · realistic AI UGC drama · talking head (TikTok storytime style) · paper / cardboard animation · Lego animation · long-form static.

Concepts named on the call

  • Symptoms callout for older women with the super-simple mechanism, in the CB98-A shape
  • Broke-my-ankle spiral — injury → couch → weight gain → can't move to fix it. Pure quality-of-life, zero blame.
  • Realistic-AI-UGC translations of Lymphoria's animated stories (walking in on the affair, etc.) — the two Resilia ads proving the format: Amusement Park, Heart Attack Hunting
  • Talking-head storytime in the Kels Gordon style — 7–8 minutes, no visuals, pure open loop. Reference build: Batch #136 in this style
  • "Am I an asshole" Reddit stories as a story mine
  • Male: "my wife left me because I wasn't the man I used to be"; younger-guy cutting mix

The talking-head mechanic that makes those work

The first line has to make the narrator look like the villain, then the story turns and the audience realizes they were right all along. That's what drives the comment ratio.

Native ad images

Khush's rule is the image carries ~85% of the concept and should be unhinged. Mei's counter-data: the winner on both the wedding ad and Batch #136 was just a woman and her engagement ring, and the more unhinged variants underperformed. Resolution — the image must be congruent with the story, and we launch several images per ad and let Meta sort it. A high-CTR mismatched image just tanks CVR.

CTA formulas that are working

  • Risk reversal — "they have a 90-day guarantee, the risk is on them, not on you" (90 days everywhere, site included)
  • Pain contrast — "you can spend the rest of your life not fitting into your jeans, or you can drink a strawberry refresher." Mei used this in Batch #136 and it may be a big part of why it's working. Treat it as a reusable Lego piece.
  • Frame the purchase as low-stakes: worst case, it's a fun drink.

Pre-sell pages

Congruence with the ad that sent them is the single highest-impact factor, above everything else. Different mechanisms need their own pages, and segmented links come later as the funnel splits.

New post-purchase survey — direction

Full read-out: Ovia Post-Purchase Survey Report. Keep the standards (age, problem struggling with most, what made you say "yes, this is what I need"), and add:

  • Weight-gain type, including blood sugar
  • The Ogilvy claim test (Confessions, p.111): give respondents a list of benefit statements and make them pick ONE. Take big swings across the list — instant hunger stop, blood sugar, ease, willpower — not three variations of the same claim. Phrase it as a benefit they'll all get, not as a claim we're making: "Which of these benefits is most important to you?" or "Which of these would make you most likely to buy?"
  • "What's the biggest thing in your life that's been affected by your weight?" — this one is the story mine; good answers become native ads directly
  • "What have you already tried to lose weight?" (select all) — combine with mechanism
  • What almost stopped them from buying
